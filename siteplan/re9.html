The Review

Resident Evil Requiem is terrifying in the best way possible. Capcom has taken everything that made the modern RE era great and cranked it to eleven — the tension, the atmosphere, the grotesque creativity of the enemy design. From the first hour, the game grabs you by the throat and does not let go.

Leon and Ashley's dynamic hits different this time around. The writing is sharper, the scares are earned, and the level design is some of the most claustrophobic, brilliant work I've seen in the series. Every corner you turn could be your last — and the game knows exactly how to make you feel that.

Graphically, it's stunning. Lighting, particle effects, the grotesque detail on the enemies — this is what next-gen horror looks like. The sound design deserves a separate award entirely. Play this with headphones. Seriously.

What separates Requiem from its predecessors isn't just the polish — it's the confidence. Capcom knows exactly what it wants this game to be, and it executes that vision without compromise or hesitation. Every room feels authored. Every scare is intentional. This is horror as a craft.

Final Verdict

"Resident Evil Requiem is a masterclass in horror game design. Capcom has delivered the definitive modern RE experience — a game that terrifies, thrills, and lingers long after the credits roll. An essential play for any horror fan, and one of the best games of 2025."

The final act twist — which I won't name but you'll know exactly when it hits — is one of the most gut-punch moments in Resident Evil history. The way Requiem ties its narrative back to earlier games while still feeling fresh is a masterclass in franchise continuity. The ending left me genuinely stunned and immediately wanting a sequel.
